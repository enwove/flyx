# Tree rounds of more than 16 rows at one-row bits: what a verify pass costs on MiniCPM5-1B (2026-11-17)

RTX 5060 Ti 8 GB (8151 MiB; the desktop's display GPU, 1.7 GB held by other programs during the first runs, 2.5 to
3.0 GB during the last), Windows 11 Home 26300, driver 610.88, MSVC 19.44, CUDA 13.5; a release build
(`release-windows` with `FLYX_TESTS=ON`, `FLYX_CUDA_ARCHS=120-real`) of flyx-windows 4ae5ae7 with this change (ABI 32:
tree rounds of up to 64 rows, every GEMM 16 rows at a time,
[tree-verification.md](../design/tree-verification.md#rounds-of-more-than-16-rows-phase-2)).
Raw output: [2026-11-17-rtx5060ti-tree-rows.txt](2026-11-17-rtx5060ti-tree-rows.txt).  The timings and the profile
(its sections 1 and 2) ran before the change's review fixes, which changed no round's work (an engine lowers no round
of more than 16 rows of its own, a session leases what it takes, tests); the memory, rule 4 and "unchanged" sections
ran on the final code (section 6), with one timing run beside them.

Model: `minicpm5-1b.flyx`, MiniCPM5-1B in BF16 (decision 0074), binary16 K/V (`--kv 16`). Rounds of given drafts
(`bench/tree/tree_bench.cpp`): a prompt of random tokens, then rounds of random drafts (nearly none accepted, so
every round moves one position and its rows' work is all of its cost), two in flight, graphs, as the engine runs
them; a round of w drafts verifies w + 1 rows.

```
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024 --widths 15,31,47,63 --rounds 100     # three times
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 4096 --widths 15,31,47,63 --rounds 100
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024 --widths 3,7,15,31 --rounds 100       # a session of 32 rows
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024 --widths 15,31,63 --rounds 30 --backend-options profile=1
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024|9000 --widths 3,7,15,31 --check 400
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024 --widths 31 --check 400
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024 --widths 47,63 --check 400
tree_bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --kv 16 --ctx-at 1024 --widths 31,63 --check 400 \
    --backend-options rows_attention_from=0      # and fusion=0
```

## A round by its rows

ms a round (the tree a chain; a random tree of the same rows 1 to 2% more), mean of the three runs at 1024 tokens of
context (they agree within 0.4%), and one run at 4096:

| rows | 1 (plain) | 16 | 32 | 48 | 64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ms, context 1024 | 4.331 | 6.362 | 13.101 | 20.139 | 27.583 |
| t(rows) / t(1) | 1 | 1.469 | **3.025** | 4.650 | 6.368 |
| ms, context 4096 | 4.633 | 8.281 | 16.774 | 25.076 | 33.962 |
| t(rows) / t(1) | 1 | 1.787 | 3.621 | 5.412 | 7.330 |

The run on the final code (section 6; another program held 2.9 GB of the GPU then) gave 4.502 ms a plain round and
2.97, 4.54 and 6.46 plain rounds at 32, 48 and 64 rows (a chain; the other shapes up to 9% more), and 3.19 at 32 rows in
the run after it: the same costs within that GPU's noise.

t(32) / t(1) is 3.0, not the 1.9 to 2.1 the plan estimated: the estimate took a 16-row tile for about the cost of a
plain round (the weights read once), and the 16-row round itself costs 1.47 of one here. Past 16 rows the cost is
the 16-row round's again for every 16 rows (t(32) = 2.06 t(16), t(64) = 4.34 t(16)): every tile reads the weights
again and does its own work.

Where a round's time goes (option `profile=1`: no graphs, every node timed, per pass summed over the 24 layers;
plain rounds 4.98 ms without graphs against 4.33 with them):

| node (ms a pass) | 1 row | 16 rows | 32 rows | 64 rows |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| attention mixer (q\|k\|v and o GEMMs, attention) | 1.562 | 2.596 | 5.096 | 10.010 |
| mlp (gate\|up and down GEMMs) | 2.813 | 3.181 | 6.529 | 12.943 |
| head and choice (W12, or the certified head: greedy) | 0.526 | 1.042 | 2.091 | 4.187 |
| the rest (embedding, given drafts, accept and compaction) | 0.075 | 0.070 | 0.081 | 0.098 |
| a pass | 4.976 | 6.890 | 13.796 | 27.237 |

A 16-row tile adds about 1.9 ms to what one row costs (attention 1.0, MLP 0.4, head 0.5): the three bf16 parts' MMAs
of 16 rows, the attention one row a block (a tree's rows each read their own keys). A multi-tile GEMM that reads the
weights once for every tile (the plan's next step) keeps that work for every tile, so at 32 rows about
4.3 + 2 x 1.9 = 8 ms (1.9 plain rounds) is what it can reach, by this profile: the plan's 1.1 to 1.3 needs less work
a tile as well (fewer MMAs, the attention's rows together), not only fewer weight reads.

## Memory

A session's round buffers hold the widest round plan its backend lowered: rows, logits and sampling scratch (`cz`,
`ci`: the vocabulary of 130,560 floats and ints a row), attention partials, and 16 K/V slots more past the context
for every 16 rows. `seq_bytes` of a session of 49,536 tokens (section 6): 1254.99 MB with the plans of the load (16
rows), 1290.48 MB once trees of 31 drafts are lowered (32 rows), 1361.17 MB with trees of 63 (64 rows): 35.5 and 35.3
MB for every 16 rows. An engine lowers no round of more than 16 rows of its own (its given trees are of 3, 7 and 15
drafts; DV's trees of 31 will be its drafter's own), so its sessions are what they were before this change; on the
8 GB card those 35 MB would be about 1,400 positions of a fitted context (24.6 KB a position). `tree_bench` and
golden_test lower their wider trees after the load, and the sessions they make after it lease the larger size
(`Engine::session_bytes()` asks the backend at every session), so the ledger refuses one that does not fit rather
than the GPU running short.

## Rule 4 on the real model

`tree_bench --check 400`: tree rounds that hide plain decoding's continuation on a random path of a random tree (the
other drafts never the token chosen at their depth) give plain decoding's tokens and accept exactly that path (on the
final code, section 6; the runs before the review fixes, section 3, gave the same):

| trees (drafts) | context | options | rounds | tokens equal | hidden path accepted |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3, 7, 15, 31 | 1024 | | 105 | 405 of 405 | 105 of 105 |
| 3, 7, 15, 31 | 9000 (rows past 8192 keys on `attention_rows_mma`) | | 105 | 405 of 405 | 105 of 105 |
| 31 | 1024 | | 70 | 404 of 404 | 70 of 70 |
| 47, 63 | 1024 | | 53 | 400 of 400 | 53 of 53 |
| 31, 63 | 1024 | `rows_attention_from=0` | 57 | 401 of 401 | 57 of 57 |
| 31, 63 | 1024 | `fusion=0` | 57 | 401 of 401 | 57 of 57 |

The first run of trees of 47 and 63 drafts (before the fix in this change) gave plain decoding's tokens but
accepted the hidden path in 22 of 64 rounds: `given_drafts` copied the launch's drafts with one thread a draft, 32
of them, so drafts past the 32nd kept the round before's. It now loops over the drafts (and golden_test's trees of
47 and 63 drafts fail without that).

## Rounds of up to 16 rows: unchanged

GSM8K's first 50 questions as the [plain record](2026-11-17-rtx5060ti-minicpm5-gsm8k-plain.md) runs them, on the
final code (section 6), each record compared with `bench/ab/same_runs.py` (the same answers and, round by round, the
same rounds over the prompts both records hold):

```
SUFFIX='\nPlease reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}.'
flyx bench minicpm5-1b.flyx --prompts gsm8k_test.jsonl --suffix "$SUFFIX" --kv 16 --ctx 2048 --no-lookup \
    --limit 50 --json plain50.json
flyx bench minicpm5-1b-dv.flyx --prompts gsm8k_test.jsonl --suffix "$SUFFIX" --kv 16 --ctx 2048 --drafter dv \
    --limit 50 --json dv50.json
python -I bench/ab/same_runs.py plain50.json plain.json    # the plain record's run (sha256 a7eb7f26...)
python -I bench/ab/same_runs.py dv50.json dv_pool15.json   # the DV record's run of minicpm5-1b-dv.flyx (81b96763...)
```

Plain decoding gives the plain record's answers and rounds (50 of 50, 14,584 rounds), and DV's rounds of 15 drafts
are the [DV record](2026-11-17-rtx5060ti-minicpm5-gsm8k-dv15.md)'s round for round (50 of 50, 2,990 rounds, every
one's tokens and accepted drafts; 4.864 tokens a round). The records' sha256 are in the DV record's raw output.

Plain decoding's speed against the commit this change starts from, 4ae5ae7 (`git archive 4ae5ae7`, built the same
way: `release-windows` with `FLYX_CUDA_ARCHS=120-real`), the plain command above with `--json ab_{old,new}_K.json`,
the two builds taking turns (pairs 1 and 3 the old build first, 2 and 4 the new), decode tok/s:

| pair | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4ae5ae7 | 225.1 | 228.5 | 228.7 | 227.0 | 227.8 |
| this change | 222.1 | 227.5 | 228.6 | 228.6 | 228.1 |

The per-pair difference's median is -0.6 tok/s (-0.2%), the first pair (the GPU's first minutes) the widest; each
pair's two records have the same answers and rounds (same_runs.py, section 6). The plain record's 232 tok/s and the
DV record's 238 were measured earlier the same day with less of the GPU held by other programs.
