# Verifying a tree of drafts in one round

Status: phase 1 built (2026-11-13); phase 2's rounds of up to 64 rows at one-row bits built for attention-only
models (2026-11-17, ABI 32, [below](#rounds-of-more-than-16-rows-phase-2)); gated delta nets past 16 rows and phase
3 are designs. The user's own drafting method (DV: a block of mask slots filled
in one pass, a selector growing a dynamic tree of 31 to 127 nodes from their candidates) plugs in as a drafter that
drafts trees ([dv.md](dv.md)); this note is the engine's side: what a round needs to verify a tree, how others do
it, and why flyx does it differently.

## What a tree round is

A round's drafts are `token[i]` with `parent[i]` (-1: the round's anchor, row 0), parents before children. Row i
sits at position `base + depth[i]`; it attends to every committed key and to its ancestors (and itself), not to its
siblings or cousins. The target's choice at each row is computed as for any row; acceptance walks from the anchor to
the child whose token is the parent's choice, as deep as that goes, and emits the choices along that path. The
accepted path becomes the sequence: its K/V in consecutive slots, its recurrent state the committed state. A chain is
the tree with `parent[i] = i - 1`.

## How others do it (2023-2026)

* Trees on attention-only models: SpecInfer (token trees, ancestor attention mask, multi-step speculative
  sampling), Medusa (fixed trees, typical acceptance), EAGLE-2 (dynamic trees from the drafter's confidence),
  Sequoia (tree size and shape chosen from measured verification cost by tree size: the hardware-aware budget),
  DDTree / DARTree (trees from a block-diffusion drafter's per-slot distributions, best-first under a node budget).
* Hybrid models (recurrent layers keep one state, which a tree's branches cannot share):
  * vLLM and SGLang by default keep a full state snapshot per draft position and serve Qwen3-Next / Qwen3.5 with
    chains only (topk 1); ReplaySSM (SGLang opt-in, a vLLM RFC) keeps a checkpoint and the window's inputs and
    replays at commit, chains only. flyx's commit already works this way (the round's replay buffer, folded).
  * STree (arXiv 2505.14969): trees for Mamba-2, whose scalar decay accumulates along paths.
  * TreeWY (arXiv 2608.20961): a tree-structured WY form of the gated delta rule, one triangular solve over the
    nodes with an ancestor mask, per-node pseudo-values instead of states, the accepted state rebuilt at commit;
    equal to the recurrence to bf16 rounding, not bitwise; trees raised acceptance (3.23 -> 3.58 at 39 nodes, depth
    3) but were not a throughput gain.
  * Bole (arXiv 2608.01651): the tree's closed form solved by a finite Neumann series to the tree's depth, compact
    factors per node, 82-99% less transient state; "algebraically exact", no floating-point identity; sizes trees by
    the largest node count whose forward stays within (1 + eps) of a plain decode step.
  * LumoTree (arXiv 2609.23900): keeps the sequential updates, runs paths in parallel from states cut at branch
    points, replays the accepted path at commit, gathers conv histories along ancestry; puts the main path's K/V
    contiguously; still five greedy disagreements at exact logit ties in 126 cycles: not bitwise.
  * SpecLA (arXiv 2607.16673): topology-aware kernels and compact factors for linear-attention models.

None of them gives a tree-verified token the bits plain decoding gives it. flyx's rule 4 (AGENTS.md: speculation
never changes the output) needs exactly that, so:

## flyx's design: every node's arithmetic is plain decoding's

1. **The launch's tree** (`flyx_gen_params`: `drafts`, `n_drafts`, and new `parents`): the way an outside drafter
   (the user's method, lookup) hands a round its drafts. Rounds with `FLYX_NODE_GIVEN` take a tree; the built-in
   drafters' rounds stay chains with their plans and kernels unchanged. The launch derives each row's depth and its
   path (the row of its ancestor at every depth) once, on the host, into the round's device tables.
2. **Positions by depth**: rotary embedding of row i at `base + depth[i]`; its K/V written to slot `base + i`.
3. **Attention in the order decoding would see the keys**: row i's logical keys are the committed ones, then its
   path by depth; key `j >= base` is read from slot `base + path[i][j - base]`. Tiles and key splits follow logical
   positions, as in decoding, so the sums are the same sums in the same order. In tree plans the grouped kernels
   take one row a block (a row's bits do not depend on the rows beside it), since rows of different branches cannot
   share a tile of their own part; the tensor-core kernel (from 8192 keys) keeps its rows together: they share the
   tiles before the round's keys and take the tiles holding them one row at a time.
4. **GDN layers walk the tree**: the short convolution of row i takes the inputs of its last three ancestors (the
   committed window above the anchor); the recurrence goes through the rows in order, and where a row's parent is
   not the row before it, it reloads the round's starting state and replays the parent's path (the same per-row
   operations), then goes on. Rows of up to 16: a replay is a few steps. (Larger trees: keep branch states in shared
   memory or L2 instead of replaying; phase 2.) The fused one-kernel round stays for chains; tree plans take the four
   kernels, which give the same bits.
5. **Choices**: a row's sampled choice draws from its position's random stream (`base + depth[i]`), as decoding at
   that position does; penalties count the row's ancestors' drafts. So a tree round's choices, sampling included, are
   the ones plain decoding makes along each path, and acceptance stays exact matching.
6. **Accept** walks the tree from the anchor and writes the accepted path's rows.
7. **Compact, then commit**: the accepted path's rows are moved to rows 0..n everywhere a later step reads them (K/V
   in every attention layer, the GDN replay rows, the drafters' input rows); then the commit and the drafters'
   context run as after a chain round.
8. **Relaxed acceptance (opt-in, lossy)**: min-p (accept a child if the target gives its token at least tau times
   its top probability; the DV method's "DVC" rule, tau 0.2) and typical acceptance (Medusa: probability above min(eps,
   sqrt(eps) exp(-entropy)), eps 0.09); at a node the qualifying child with the highest target probability; the
   round ends with the target's own choice. Off by default.

## What phase 1 is, in the code

* ABI 23: `flyx_gen_params.parents` (and `accept_min_p`, `accept_typical`), `FLYX_GIVEN_TREE` as the GIVEN
  node's arg1.  The core lowers tree rounds of 3, 7 and 15 drafts where the backend has them
  (`Engine::tree_widths()`, `tree_plan()`); cpu_ref has none (it refuses the plan), the CUDA backend all.  Wider
  given trees (ABI 32, below) are lowered by what runs them (golden_test, tree_bench), not by the engine.
* The launch turns the parents into `TreeRows` (every row's depth and its path) on the host and copies it through a
  ring of pinned slots, as the drafts; a round's graph reads the table, so one captured graph serves every tree of
  its width.
* Kernels: `attn_prep` (position by depth, slot by row), the three attention kernels (`tree_slot`; the float32 ones
  one row a block, the tensor-core one sharing the keys before the round among its rows),
  the combine (splits by the row's own key count), `gdn_conv` (ancestors' inputs), `gdn_recur_tree` (the lanes
  kernel's TREE instance: replays where a row's parent is not the row before), `penalize_rows` (ancestors' drafts),
  `choose_rows` (depth's random stream), `accept_tree` (ops::accept_tree), and the compactions (`compact_kv`,
  `gdn_compact`, `compact_rows`: K/V slots and e4m3 scales, replay rows, tokens, final hidden rows, taps).  The
  fused one-kernel GDN round is not used in tree plans.
* Relaxed acceptance runs in any round with drafts (chains too) when the request asks: `row_stats` after the head,
  `accept_tree` with the rules; its rounds get graphs of their own.

Tests (golden_test, CUDA, every golden configuration: BF16, NVFP4, unfused, GDN 128, tensor-core attention from
key 0, the tiny Qwen3 in BF16 and GGUF): random trees of 3, 7 and 15 drafts hide plain decoding's continuation on a
random path (half of them the deepest), the other drafts never the token chosen at their depth; every round must
emit plain decoding's tokens and accept exactly the hidden path, 20 rounds a run: greedy, sampling, penalties, FP8
caches, min-p 1 (only the top token qualifies: plain decoding again).  The MTP drafter's rounds after tree rounds
whose paths were moved give the ring entries (drafts too) of the same rounds whose paths were already in place.
Breaking the compaction or the GDN replay fails them at the first moved path.  Chains: the existing golden tests,
unchanged.

What phase 1 costs a round: the float32 attention one row a block where a chain shares a block among rows (the 27B's
takes one row a block anyway), GDN replays (at most the depth of a row, a few steps), the four GDN kernels instead of the fused
one, and the compactions (a few KB a layer).

## A drafter's own trees (DFlash2)

Given trees test the verification; a drafter that drafts trees uses it.  DFlash2 already scores 16 candidates at
each of its 7 positions (the target's head on the drafter's rows) and a low-rank selector rates each candidate after
the token before it; its walk keeps the best chain.  `FLYX_DRAFT_TREE` (ABI 24, the DRAFT node's arg1) asks for a
tree instead (ops/draft/ref.h): the walk's chain first (the trunk), then branches grown best first, EAGLE-2's dynamic
tree on these scores (a node's value the sum of the log-probabilities along its path, each the selector's softmax
over a parent's 16 candidates; every further draft the highest-valued child of a node already in the tree).  The
kernel (`selector_tree`, one block) writes the drafts and the round's `TreeRows`, so the launch uploads no tree and
one graph serves every tree of a width; the round is then a tree round as above, and its result names the tree
(`parents`, ABI 24).  The MTP layer drafts one token a step and has no trees (the backend refuses them).

Why the trunk: a tree grown best first from the anchor alone accepted fewer drafts on the 27B than the chain of 7
(the selector's softmax is far less sure than the drafter is: the chain's later drafts are accepted often), so the
tree is the chain and what its budget adds; it never accepts less than its chain.

Which round runs is the speculation controller's choice, sized to the GPU by measured costs: with `--draft-trees`
(FLYX_DRAFT_TREES=1) the plans add DFlash2 trees of 9, 11 and 15 drafts, each timed at load like every round.  A
tree's expected tokens are its trunk's (the drafter's acceptance by position, which every tree round teaches as well,
its trunk being the chain) plus what its branches add (learned per tree size from the tokens its rounds gave beyond
their trunk's, 0.4 until seen; each tree tried once in 64 of its drafter's rounds); a tree runs where that over its
cost beats every chain.  Measured on the RTX 5090 (docs/perf/2026-11-13-rtx5090-drafted-trees.md,
decision 0048): the branches add 0.15-0.23 tokens each for the first two, a row past 8 costs more than that, so the
chains win and the option is off by default.

Tests: `selector_tree` against best_first_tree in test_select (its trunk the walk's chain; a tree that must branch,
one of a full round, one shallower than its positions); golden: DFlash2's trees taking turns with its chains give the
reference's tokens, with oracle drafts the long accepted runs, chosen every round too, sampled plain decoding's
tokens for one seed, and a session continued from inside its last rounds; round_chooser: the tree where its
branches add tokens, the chain where they add nothing.

## Phases

1. Trees of up to 16 rows, end to end (above).
2. Rounds of more than 16 rows at one-row bits (the round GEMMs, attention and GDN for 31-255 rows), and branch
   states instead of replays.  Built for attention-only models up to 64 rows (below); GDN to do.
3. (Later, if the user's method needs it) rows in the same pass that are not verified: their own mask, starting
   state and weights. DV's canvas runs as a pass of its own after the check first (option A); fusing its rows into
   the verify pass is this phase (option B, [dv.md](dv.md)).

## Rounds of more than 16 rows (phase 2)

Built (ABI 32), for models of attention layers (a Llama, Qwen3; GDN models keep trees of 16 rows, refused at
lowering, so their plans lower as before):

* **Limits.** Option (b) below: `FLYX_MAX_TREE_ROWS` (64) rows for tree rounds and `FLYX_MAX_EMIT` (64) tokens,
  drafts and parents in a ring entry; chains, rounds of several sequences and `flyx_room` keep `FLYX_MAX_ROWS` (16).
  The core's emission buffer and the speculation controller's positions follow `FLYX_MAX_EMIT`; tree plans lower up
  to 64 rows.  `TreeRows` holds 64 rows (4.6 KB), the attention kernel's path in shared memory 64 bytes, the
  penalties' and the accept's arrays 64.
* **Buffers** follow the widest round plan the backend lowered (`flyx_bmodel::round_rows`, 16 until a wider one):
  a session's round rows, logits, sampling scratch (`cz`, `ci`), attention partials, drafts and accept statistics;
  the GEMM scratch stays the 16-row one plus bf16 rows for `gemm_fp4_rows`.  On MiniCPM5-1B (vocabulary 130,560)
  that is 35 MB a session for every 16 rows past 16 (`seq_bytes`), so an engine lowers no round of more than 16
  rows of its own (`plan::tree_widths()` stays 3, 7 and 15; a drafter whose rounds are wider, DV's trees of 31,
  will lower its own): its sessions are as before.  A wider plan lowered after the load through the ABI (golden_test,
  tree_bench) makes the sessions made after it larger, and each leases what the backend says it takes then
  (`Engine::session_bytes()` asks `seq_size` at every session, so the ledger refuses one that does not fit rather
  than the GPU running out); a session made before a wider plan refuses its rounds.
* **The end of the context, one rule.** `flyx_room` is unchanged: a round starts only where one of 16 rows fits.
  A round emits at most `flyx_emit_most(position, max_ctx) = max_ctx - position` tokens (`ops::accept_tree` cuts
  its walk there; a round of 16 rows or fewer never reaches it), so no token is ever past `max_ctx`, and the
  sequence stops there.  Its rows past `max_ctx` are verified in K/V slots kept beyond it: `cap = max_ctx + 16 + R`
  for R round rows (`max_ctx + 32` before, the same for R = 16), enough for the round in flight after it too.
* **GEMMs.** A round's pass carries `Launch::round`; with it a GEMM of more than 16 rows runs the 1..16-row
  choice 16 rows at a time (bf16: `gemm_skinny` per tile, `gemm_rows` with option `gemm=rows`; GGML blocks: a
  round's staging and kernels per tile), NVFP4 through `gemm_fp4_rows` (each weight span decoded once for up to
  four tiles, each tile's arithmetic the 1..16-row kernel's).  A prefill pass never has the flag.
* **Fused regions.** A fused producer stages its pass's rows whole for the next GEMM (tensor cores past 16 rows),
  so round plans of more than 16 rows resolve to the generic regions; fused and generic give the same bits (class
  F), so a row verified in such a round has the bits plain decoding's fused rounds give it.
* **The head** runs in parts of 16 rows, each part on the 1..16-row paths (W12, the FP8 copy, the certified head
  with its scratch in turn); penalties, choices and the relaxed rules' statistics over all rows.
* **Attention** keeps the split kernels for a tree (never `attention_flash`, which has no tree): the float32
  kernels one row a block, `attention_rows_mma` for a row of `rows_attention_from` keys or more, by the row's own
  key count, as decoding it does.
* **Not with it:** strata attention (its scratch holds 16 rows; such a launch is refused), rounds of several
  sequences (trees run alone), DFlash2's trees (its selector's kernel drafts 15 at most).

Checked: `cuda_ops` (`test_gemm_round`: bf16 skinny tiles, `gemm=rows` and NVFP4 at 17, 31, 32, 33, 48 and 64
rows, every row the bits it has alone, and the same rows as a prefill pass not; `test_head_parts`: W12, the FP8
copy and the certified head in parts; `test_attention_tree`: trees of 17, 32 (a chain of depth 31, a random one)
and 64 rows (a trunk of 40), every row the bits of decoding its path alone, with every kernel choice, about 8192
keys too), golden tree rounds of 31 drafts and of 47 and 63 (plans the test lowers) on every attention-only tiny
file (the Llama, its NVFP4 copy, held to its own plain decoding, the Qwen3 in BF16 and as a float32 GGUF, read as
bf16, two Qwen3 of GGML block weights, Q8_0 and Q4_K, held to their own plain decoding: `golden_cuda_quant_*`, the
DV files' targets; unfused, tensor-core attention from key 0, FP8 K/V, the K/V all in host memory; greedy, sampled,
with penalties and min-p 1; read a round at a time and two in flight, each round launched behind the one before
with its drafts where that round's path ends), tree rounds of 32 and 64 rows 16 positions before the context's end
(the round in flight after each writing its rows past it, the prompt's K/V, every layer's, the bytes they were), a
tree round of 64 rows in flight before strata attention's switch (the one launched behind it, which it may move
past the switch, refused; where the model's shape has strata kernels: the Qwen3s), and `tree_bench --check` with
trees of 31, 47 and 63 on MiniCPM5-1B ([record](../perf/2026-11-17-rtx5060ti-tree-rows.md)).  The prefill GEMM in
such a round instead fails the kernel tests and the tiny Llama's tree rounds; a tree on `attention_flash` fails both
everywhere; the GGML block GEMMs or the packed head on all of a round's rows at once fail `golden_cuda_quant_*`; a
K/V cap of `max_ctx + 32` fails the context's end at 64 rows; a ring of one pinned slot for drafts and trees fails
the rounds two in flight with the K/V in host memory; counting a round in flight as 16 rows fails the strata
switch.

What it costs (MiniCPM5-1B, BF16, `--kv 16`, RTX 5060 Ti, 1024 tokens of context;
[record](../perf/2026-11-17-rtx5060ti-tree-rows.md)): a round of 16 rows 1.47 plain rounds, 32 rows 3.03, 48 rows
4.65, 64 rows 6.37 (at 4096 tokens: 1.79, 3.62, 5.41, 7.33).  Every 16 rows cost the 16-row round again: each tile
reads the weights and adds its own work (about 1.9 ms a tile over a plain round's 4.3: the three bf16 parts' MMAs,
the attention a row a block, the head).  A multi-tile bf16 GEMM that reads the weights once for every tile saves the
reads, not that work: about 1.9 plain rounds at 32 rows by the profile.

## Phase 2: what it needs, and the open choice

Built: the NVFP4 GEMM of 17..64 rows with the 1..16-row kernel's bits (`gemm_fp4_rows`), and rounds of up to 64
rows above (the row limit by option (b), every GEMM 16 rows at a time, the head in parts, attention).  Still to
do:

* **The row limit.** FLYX_MAX_ROWS (16) sizes the ABI's ring entries (tokens, drafts), the per-session row
  buffers and logits, TreeRows, and the room every pass leaves at the end of the context (flyx_room). Two ways:
  (a) raise it for every round (64 or 128): one constant, but every session's buffers grow (a 64-row round's logits
  alone are 64 MB at the 27B's vocabulary) and every sequence keeps that many positions free at its end; (b) keep 16
  for rounds as they are and give tree plans a limit of their own (FLYX_MAX_TREE_ROWS), their buffers allocated
  when a session first runs one, their ring entry a separate result. (b) leaves everything that runs today as it is.
  Taken: (b), with one ring entry of FLYX_MAX_EMIT for every round (ABI 32) and the buffers sized when a session is
  made, by the widest round plan lowered (allocated on first use, `seq_bytes` would not count them).
* **GEMMs.** Done 16 rows at a time (bf16, GGML blocks; NVFP4 four tiles a weight decode).  To do: the bf16
  multi-tile form of k_gemm_skinny (the weights read once for every tile).
* **Attention.** The split kernels take any number of rows; a tree's round keys beyond one 16-key tile are already
  read through tree_slot. The tensor-core kernel takes the tiles of round keys one row at a time: with 64 rows, the
  last 4 tiles 64 times; a deeper tree wants its rows grouped by branch.
* **GDN.** The recurrence stages 16 rows at a time; a row's replay must read ancestors staged earlier (from the
  replay buffer in global memory instead of shared memory), and replays grow with depth: past 16 rows, branch states
  kept for the tree's branch points (a few states a head in L2) instead of replays from the round's start.
* **Head and choices.** Done: the head 16 rows at a time into the round's logits, penalties, choices and the
  relaxed rules' statistics over all rows; the accept walk reads the choices of all rows.
* **Verification in 4-bit inputs (an option to decide).** Rounds that keep plain decoding's bits are bound by the
  bf16 tensor cores (float32 accumulation runs at half rate on GeForce): 2.9 plain rounds at 32 rows, 5.1 at 64 on
  the 27B (perf record). The W4A4 kernels (NVFP4 inputs, the prefill GEMM) cost 2.2 at 32 and about 2.4 at 64, at
  llama.cpp's precision for passes of that many rows, but not plain decoding's bits: lossy like the relaxed rules,
  so it could only be an option off by default.

## Is it worth verifying more rows?

A round verifying R rows costs t(R); plain decoding emits one token per t(1). The round is faster only if it emits
more than t(R) / t(1) tokens on average: the break-even line, measured on the 27B in
docs/perf/2026-11-13-rtx5090-tree-break-even.md.
