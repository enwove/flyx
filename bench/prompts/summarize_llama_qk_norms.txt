# 0074 Llama's attention without q/k norms: MiniCPM5-1B, plain decoding

Date: 2026-11-17. Status: built; MiniCPM5-1B checked against transformers and run on an RTX 5060 Ti 8 GB (Windows 11,
driver 610.88, CUDA 13.5).

## Why

The owner's drafting method, DV (a block of mask slots filled in one pass, a selector growing a tree of 31 to 127
nodes; [tree verification](../design/tree-verification.md)), is developed and evaluated on MiniCPM5-1B: its trees
reach 9.5 tokens a forward there, and its relaxed rule's cost was measured on MiniCPM5-1B's GSM8K answers
([record](../perf/2026-11-13-rtx5090-tree-break-even.md)). Before DV can plug into flyx's rounds, flyx has to run
MiniCPM5-1B itself, and give transformers' tokens.

MiniCPM5-1B is published as `LlamaForCausalLM`: 24 layers of attention (16 query heads on 2 KV heads of 128, the
whole head rotated, theta 5e6) and a SwiGLU MLP of 4608, plain RMSNorm weights, an untied head of 130560 rows, no
biases and none of the earlier MiniCPMs' scaling. That is Qwen3's layer without the per-head RMSNorm of q and k
before the rotary. Its tokenizer cuts numbers into groups of up to three before a Qwen2-like split.

## Decision

1. **The model describes the absence; no new block kind.** Decision 0008 lists "q/k norms yes/no" among the
   attention block's flags, and backend_abi.h already said that an index of -1 means none. A Llama's attention
   blocks name no q/k norm (`flyx_norm` weight -1); everything else is the block Qwen3 uses. `models/qwen3`
   became `models/llama`, one description for the family: arch `llama` (no q/k norms) and arch `qwen3` (q/k
   norms), the norms decided by the architecture, not by which tensors a file happens to hold (a Llama file with
   q/k norm tensors is refused). A second copy of qwen3.cpp for a one-field difference, or a `qk_norm` key in the
   file's config, would each be a second way to say what the architecture already says.
2. **The backends take "none" as the identity before the rotary.** The float64 reference is `ops::prep_head` (a
   head's norm where there is one, then the rotary), which cpu_ref and the kernel tests use. CUDA's attention prep
   (`k_attn_prep`) takes null norm pointers: no sum, and in its place the barrier the sum's barriers were
   (a block can hold several heads; the next head's write of the shared row must wait for the last one's reads).
   Blocks with norms compute as before: Qwen3's and Qwen3.5's logits keep their bits (below). The CUDA prep
   takes one offset and eps for both norms, so it takes q and k norms both or neither (`supported()` refuses one
   without the other).
3. **The ABI keeps its number (30).** The structures did not change, and a backend built before fails on a block
   without q/k norms ("weight -1 missing": CUDA's at open, cpu_ref's in the first pass) rather than computing
   anything else. A new number would make every engine refuse every older backend, for models those backends run
   correctly.
4. **MiniCPM5's pre-tokenizer.** The converter names the pipeline `minicpm5` (tokenizer.json: no normalizer, a
   Split on `\p{N}{1,3}`, then Qwen2's split with `\p{N}+`, then the byte-level step) and core/tokenizer implements
   it: runs of numbers cut into threes from the left, then the word split on each piece by itself (a piece's end is
   the end of the text for `\s+(?!\S)`: "  12" splits as "  ", "12", not " ", " ", "12").
   Its post-processor (TemplateProcessing) puts `<s>` (id 0) before every text, whatever `add_bos_token` in
   tokenizer_config.json says: transformers' `tok("hi")` is `[0, 7466]`. The converter records the tokens a
   post-processor puts before the text (header `tokenizer.prefix_ids`, each a special token) and refuses every
   other shape (a token after the text, BERT's and RoBERTa's processors); a ByteLevel post-processor (Qwen's) only
   moves offsets. The engine puts them before every raw prompt (`/v1/completions`, `flyx run` without `--chat`,
   the C API and the binding's text, `flyx tokenize`, `kld`): `render_request` writes them as text, each token in
   a span of its own, as a template writes its `<s>`, so a session's history (its decoded text) is still continued
   by the client's text. A chat prompt has its `<s>` from the template.
5. **The converter** maps `model_type` `llama` (and `minicpm5`) to arch `llama` and refuses what this Llama does not
   compute: biases, another activation, scaled rotary (whichever key names its kind: `rope_type`, or the older
   `type` that many Llama-family configs still write and transformers reads as the same; any `factor` other than
   1), sliding windows, and the scale factors other Llama-family checkpoints carry (MiniCPM 1 to 4's `scale_emb`,
   `scale_depth`, `dim_model_base`; Granite's multipliers). The end tokens are the model's `</s>` and
   `<|im_end|>` (1, 130073).
6. **Chat through the model's own template.** The Jinja engine renders MiniCPM5's template as transformers does (a
   test fixture now, `tests/core/render/templates/minicpm5.jinja`). Its tool calls are `<function name=...>` with
   `<param>` children and no `<tool_call>` around them, which no reply format here reads: the template is
   recognised as `plain` (thinking blocks read, tools refused), not mistaken for Qwen's `hermes` or `qwen3_coder`.
7. **Binary16 K/V by default on a Llama.** Rule 5 takes a default rounding coarser than llama.cpp's only where a
   real model showed no visible drop, and on MiniCPM5-1B FP8 K/V moves the next tokens visibly (below), where
   decision 0061 measured none on Qwen. The cache's default is the model's now (`interfaces::Model::
   kv_fp8_by_default`): FP8 for Qwen3.5 and Qwen3 as before, binary16 for arch `llama`. `--kv`, the C API's
   `FLYX_KV_FP8` / `FLYX_KV_16`, the binding's `kv=` and `POST /v1/load`'s `"kv"` still choose either; without
   them (`FLYX_KV_DEFAULT`, `kv=None`, the dashboard's first choice) the model's holds, and the engine's options
   carry the one taken after the load.

## Measured

MiniCPM5-1B in BF16 (`flyx_convert --model MiniCPM5-1B -o minicpm5-1b.flyx`: 2.16 GB, 3 s), RTX 5060 Ti 8 GB:

* **Against transformers** (`tests/e2e/check_real_model.py --hf MiniCPM5-1B --model minicpm5-1b.flyx --drafters
  --hf-dtype float32`: four chat prompts without thinking, 128 tokens, binary16 K/V, no drafter; transformers on
  the CPU): greedy tokens identical to transformers in float32 on all four (22, 126, 128 and 128 tokens, up to the
  end of the turn). Against transformers in bfloat16 (the same command without `--hf-dtype float32`, the script's
  default), two identical; the other two first differ at tokens 17 and 63, where bfloat16's top two logits are
  0.000 and 0.125 apart (a tie, and one bfloat16 step).
* **Tokenizer**: the script's 10 texts, alone and as raw prompts (`<s>` first, as transformers' `tok(text)`), and
  3690 more (the model's READMEs, flyx's decisions, C++ and Python, and 3000 runs of numbers in five scripts beside
  spaces, letters and punctuation; 187,760 tokens), every one tokenized as HuggingFace tokenizers does.
* **Template**: every conversation of `chat_cases.py` without tools renders to transformers' text, branch points
  and thinking block; with tools, refused.
* **Speed** (`flyx bench minicpm5-1b.flyx -n 128`: its three short prompts, greedy, FP8 K/V): 231.1 tok/s plain
  (`--no-lookup`; 232.1 with `--kv 16`), 268.5 tok/s by default (lookup rounds: 1.17 tokens a round); prefill of 33
  to 84 tokens 7.6 to 8.9 ms; load 1.3 s, 2.28 GB of weights on the GPU. `flyx run --chat --no-think` answers two
  GSM8K-style questions right ($639 and 37 pencils) at 232.6 and 229.9 tok/s.
* **FP8 K/V** (0061's default, `--kv fp8` here) is coarser here than where 0061 measured it: against binary16 (`kld
  minicpm5-1b.flyx --text ... --tokens 400 --start 32 --kv 16 --save ref.bin`, then `--kv fp8 --ref ref.bin`: 400
  positions after 32 tokens of each of the model's two READMEs and tree-verification.md, each text after the `<s>`
  its tokenizer puts first) it gives KL 0.0064 (p99 0.063), the same top-1 97.2%, perplexity 14.78 against 14.58
  (+1.4%), where the 27B gave 0.0009 to 0.0016, 98.6% and 0.1%. (Measured first without the `<s>`, on token
  streams the model does not see in use: KL 0.0082, 95.6%, +1.1%.) Keys that no norm has evened out keep their
  large channels, and one e4m3 scale per position and head leaves the others few bits. `check_real_model.py --kv-fp8
  --drafters --hf-dtype float32`: two prompts still transformers' float32 tokens (22 and 128); the planets' list
  has them for 35 tokens and then ends its turn where float32 goes on (the script compares up to the engine's end
  of turn, so it reports "identical over 35 tokens"); the Chinese one first differs at token 9 (top-2 gap 0.405).
  Binary16 costs this model nothing at these lengths (232.1 against 231.1 tok/s) and about 12 KB a token more: 24 KB
  a token against FP8's 12.4 KB (a byte a value and a float32 scale per position and head, for K and for V, in 24
  layers of 2 heads of 128).
* **Bits kept**: the log-probabilities of Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3.5-4B NVFP4 (`kld --save`, 200 positions of a text)
  are byte for byte those of the build before this change.
* **Tests**: 65 of 65, six of them new (`tokenizer_minicpm5`, `golden_cpu_ref_llama`, `golden_cuda_llama` with
  its unfused and tensor-core variants: a tiny Llama of MiniCPM5's head shape, golden tokens from transformers in
  float64; `converter`: scaled rotary refused under either key, the post-processor's prefix written and other
  post-processors refused). The tiny Llama's tokenizer has MiniCPM5's post-processor: `tokenizer_minicpm5` holds a
  raw prompt's tokens to transformers' `tok(text)`, and `request` holds a raw prompt on it to them, the session's
  history still kept. `gguf_models` refuses an unknown architecture and a Llama GGUF, each by its own message.
  `golden_*_llama` and `golden_*_qwen3` check the cache each takes when not told (binary16, FP8).
  The kernel tests' cases without q/k norms, MiniCPM5's shape (decode, 16 rows, a 100-row prefill chunk whose
  blocks hold three heads each, the FP8 cache): q within 2.1e-7 of float64, every "same bits" check exact.

## Not done

* **A drafter for MiniCPM5-1B** (DV, or another): plain decoding only; lookup rounds (0024) still draft from the
  session's own text.
* **MiniCPM5's tool calls** read back (and their grammar for `tool_choice`): a request with tools is refused.
* **A Llama GGUF**: llama.cpp's converter permutes a Llama's q and k rows for its interleaved rotary pairs; reading
  that would take a row order in the description (as Qwen3.5's gate rows have), not done.
* **A GGUF's `add_bos_token`** is still refused by the GGUF reader rather than taken as a prefix: no GGUF this build
  reads needs one.
